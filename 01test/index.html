Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship refers to the responsible, ethical, and safe use of technology and online spaces. It's essentially a framework for how people should navigate the digital world, covering everything from how you interact with others online to how you protect your own privacy and data.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law refers to the legal rules and regulations that govern behavior, transactions, and rights in online and digital spaces. It's essentially how existing legal principles (and new ones created specifically for tech) get applied to things like the internet, computers, and digital data.

What is AUP?

AUP stands for Acceptable Use Policy. It's a document, usually set by a school, employer, internet service provider, or other organization, that outlines the rules for how people are allowed to use a network, device, or online service.

What is plagiarism?

Plagiarism is using someone else's words, ideas, work, or creative output and presenting them as your own without proper credit or permission.

What is academic dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is a broader term than plagiarism - it refers to any form of dishonest or unethical behavior related to academic work that violates the standards of honesty and integrity expected in an educational setting. Plagiarism is one specific type of academic dishonesty, but there are several others.

What are the rules for AI generated content?

AI-generated content rules mostly come down to context and disclosure: in school, it depends entirely on what your professor's syllabus says, ranging from a full ban to allowed-with-citation to no restrictions at all, so the safest move is always to check and ask if unsure; more broadly, the general expectation is to be transparent about when and how you used AI rather than passing it off as fully your own work, especially if you're supposed to be demonstrating your own learning; and legally, content that's entirely AI-generated with no real human input generally can't be copyrighted in the U.S., since copyright still requires meaningful human authorship.
